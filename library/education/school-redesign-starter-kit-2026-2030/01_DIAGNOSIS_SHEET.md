# 01 — School Diagnosis Sheet
## Industrial labor model vs Human formation model

Use this as a 30–45 minute staff or leadership audit. Score each item 1–5 (1 = fully industrial / labor-prep; 5 = fully oriented to human formation). Discuss the gaps.

### Purpose & success metrics
- Primary success metric is standardized test scores / college placement / workforce readiness.  
- Primary success metric includes student agency, judgment, character, relationships, creativity, and ability to learn independently.

### Daily experience of students
- Age-locked cohorts, fixed periods, largely passive listening or worksheet completion.  
- Mixed-age or flexible grouping possible; high proportion of active, project, and collaborative work.

### Assessment
- High-stakes, timed, closed-book exams dominate. Cheating is mainly “copying answers.”  
- Demonstrated competence, portfolios, public exhibitions, and process evidence dominate. AI use is designed for and visible.

### Role of teachers
- Content deliverers and classroom managers measured by coverage and compliance.  
- Mentors, learning designers, guardians of social development, and coaches of judgment.

### Role of technology / AI
- Mostly used for administration, surveillance, or content delivery.  
- Used as personal tutor, co-designer of projects, and tool that students learn to direct and verify.

### Social & emotional development
- Treated as secondary or as “soft skills” add-on.  
- Treated as core curriculum: friendship, conflict, play, physical presence, moral reasoning practice.

### Equity & access
- One-size curriculum assumes similar home resources and connectivity.  
- Explicit adaptations for low-connectivity, multilingual, and disability contexts; design starts from the margins.

### Student agency
- Students have little real choice over pace, path, or demonstration method.  
- Students progressively own more of their learning path while adults hold non-negotiable human formation goals.

**Discussion prompt**: Where are we closest to 5? Where are we stuck at 1–2? What is the smallest experiment that would move one high-impact item?
