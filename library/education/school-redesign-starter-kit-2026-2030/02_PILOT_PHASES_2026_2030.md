# 02 — Pilot Phases 2026–2030
## Sequenced, low-risk experiments a principal can actually run

### Phase 0 — Foundation (1–3 months)
- Run the Diagnosis Sheet with leadership + volunteer teachers.  
- Form a small redesign team (teachers, students, parents, one community partner).  
- Choose 1–2 pilot classrooms or one grade band.  
- Establish privacy, consent, and “do no harm” rules for any AI tools.  
- Communicate clearly: this is experimentation, not a sudden system overhaul.

### Phase 1 — AI as Tutor Support (2026–2027)
- Introduce carefully chosen AI tutoring tools under teacher direction for differentiation and practice.  
- Train teachers and students in prompt quality, verification, and “when not to use AI.”  
- Begin simple portfolio collection alongside traditional assessment.  
- Protect substantial daily time for human-only interaction, play, and discussion.

### Phase 2 — Mastery & Demonstration Experiments (2027–2028)
- Replace or reduce one high-stakes exam with a public demonstration / exhibition of competence.  
- Allow students some choice of demonstration format (written, oral, project, performance).  
- Introduce cross-age mentoring or mixed-ability project teams.  
- Start measuring a small set of human-formation indicators (agency, collaboration quality, epistemic habits).

### Phase 3 — Role & Schedule Redesign (2028–2029)
- Pilot teacher role shift: more mentoring / design time, less pure content delivery.  
- Experiment with longer project blocks and reduced fixed-period fragmentation.  
- Build a simple “learning constitution” with students: rights, responsibilities, human non-negotiables.  
- Expand parent and community involvement in exhibitions and real-world projects.

### Phase 4 — System Embedding & Equity Scaling (2029–2030)
- Codify what worked into school policy and budget.  
- Design low-connectivity / offline versions of the most successful practices.  
- Share open templates with other schools.  
- Conduct honest public evaluation: what enlarged human agency, what did not, what risks emerged.

**Exit ramp rule**: Every pilot must have a clear way to stop or reverse if student well-being, equity, or learning suffers. No permanent policy change without evidence and community consent.
