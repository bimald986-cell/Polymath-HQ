# Humanity in the Machine Age — Research Ledger

This ledger separates manuscript evidence from narrative prose. Every quantitative or time-sensitive claim must be rechecked before publication.

## Evidence labels
- OBSERVED — documented historical or present condition.
- PROJECTED — forecast from an identified source; never presented as certainty.
- SCENARIO — conditional pathway constructed for this book.
- SPECULATIVE — meaningful possibility with high uncertainty.

## Current core sources

### International AI Safety Report 2025 + updates
Use for: present general-purpose AI capabilities, agent definitions, reliability limits, risk-management challenges, uncertainty of future capability trajectories.
Key discipline: report states advancement could be slow, rapid or extremely rapid; do not convert this into an AGI date.
Source: https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2025
Publications/updates: https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publications

### Stanford HAI — AI Index 2025
Use for: capability/cost/access trends and historical measurements of AI development.
Key recorded statistic: cost of inference for a system at approximately GPT-3.5 performance fell more than 280-fold between Nov 2022 and Oct 2024 according to the report's summary. Recheck against latest AI Index before publication.
Source: https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report

### International Labour Organization — Generative AI and Jobs: 2025 Update
Use for: occupational exposure, task transformation and labor-market framing.
Do not equate exposure with job elimination.

### World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs Report 2025
Use for: employer survey expectations to 2030. Treat job creation/displacement numbers as survey-based projections, not predictions.

### OECD — What should teachers teach and students learn in a future of powerful AI? (2025)
Use for: curriculum, competencies, education-system adaptation.

### UNESCO — Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research
Use for: human-centered adoption, privacy, age appropriateness and education governance.

### IEA — Energy and AI (2025)
Use for: data-center energy demand, infrastructure constraints, electricity scenarios.

### World Bank — Digital Progress and Trends Report 2025
Use for: global AI divide, connectivity, compute, context and competency.

### OpenStax World History, Second Industrial Revolution
Use for: industrialization chronology and technological transformation. Notes: first industrialization mechanized production using water/steam; second phase expanded steel, electricity, transport and communication.
Source: https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-2/pages/9-1-the-second-industrial-revolution

### Library of Congress — Industrial Revolution in the United States
Use for: U.S. industrialization, transportation, telegraph, manufacturing expansion.
Source: https://www.loc.gov/classroom-materials/industrial-revolution-in-the-united-states/

## Manuscript audit rule
Narrative chapters may use broad established history without footnote clutter in draft form. Before commercial publication, every specific date, numerical claim, quotation, disputed historical interpretation and current/future technology claim receives a source note. Original prose only; no protected charts or long source passages are to be reproduced.

## Forecast audit
Every future-facing chapter must ask:
1. What must become technically possible?
2. What must become economically affordable?
3. What infrastructure is required?
4. What laws or institutions could accelerate or prevent deployment?
5. What human preferences may resist automation even when it is possible?
6. Who benefits and who bears transition costs?
7. What evidence could falsify this scenario?

## Revision cadence
Refresh the evidence ledger before each publication edition. Fast-moving AI claims older than 12 months should be treated as candidates for re-verification.
