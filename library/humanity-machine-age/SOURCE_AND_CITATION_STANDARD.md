# Humanity in the Machine Age — Source and Citation Standard

Status: Mandatory editorial standard
Effective: 2026-10-02

## Core rule

Every externally verifiable factual claim in this book that is not common knowledge must be traceable to a source. This applies equally to historical chapters, present-day AI chapters, quantitative claims, forecasts, policy discussions, quotations, technical explanations, and claims derived from other authors or institutions.

The book must never make sourced material look like the author's original discovery, and it must never present speculation as established history or fact.

## Citation architecture

The reader-facing manuscript should remain readable. Use numbered endnotes/footnotes rather than cluttering every paragraph with raw URLs. Each chapter must contain a `Sources and Notes` section in its working manuscript. Publication layout may convert these to chapter endnotes plus a consolidated bibliography.

Each source record should capture where available:
- author(s) or responsible institution;
- title;
- publisher/journal/institution;
- publication date/year;
- edition/version when relevant;
- page/chapter/section when relevant;
- DOI, archival identifier, or stable URL;
- date accessed for mutable web sources;
- source type;
- which claim(s) it supports.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in roughly this order, depending on the claim:

1. Primary historical evidence: patents, legislation, speeches, letters, diaries, census material, archival documents, contemporary records, original scientific papers.
2. Peer-reviewed scholarship and scholarly books from reputable academic publishers.
3. Official statistical and institutional sources: national statistical agencies, ILO, OECD, UNESCO, World Bank, IMF, IEA, UN bodies, government agencies and similarly authoritative institutions.
4. High-quality research institutes and university publications.
5. Company documentation for claims specifically about that company's products, models, prices, benchmarks or policies. Company claims about broader social impact require independent corroboration where practical.
6. Reputable journalism for contemporary events when primary evidence is unavailable or when the journalistic investigation itself is relevant.
7. Secondary explainers only when stronger sources are unavailable, and never as the sole basis for an extraordinary or controversial claim.

Wikipedia, social posts, unsourced videos, AI-generated summaries and search-result snippets may be discovery aids but are not final evidence for important claims.

## Historical chapters

Historical dates, inventions, social changes and claims about earlier eras should be sourced. Where historians disagree, the manuscript should describe the disagreement rather than manufacture certainty.

Primary sources should be used where they illuminate what people at the time actually said or recorded, while modern historical scholarship should be used to avoid interpreting a primary document without context.

## AI, work, economics and education

Time-sensitive statistics must include the measurement year and relevant population or geography. Terms such as `jobs exposed`, `jobs displaced`, `tasks automated`, `productivity gain`, and `jobs affected` must not be treated as synonyms.

Forecasts must name the organization/model/scenario producing them and retain the assumptions or uncertainty necessary to interpret them.

Education claims should distinguish demonstrated learning evidence from proposed pedagogy and future scenarios. AI education recommendations should draw on research plus institutions such as UNESCO and OECD, while preserving competing evidence and legitimate uncertainty.

## Future claims

Continue the project's mandatory evidence labels:
- OBSERVED — documented as having occurred or currently existing.
- PROJECTED — an identified source has produced a forecast/projection.
- SCENARIO — our conditional exploration of a plausible pathway.
- SPECULATIVE — useful to consider but not reliably predictable.

A PROJECTED claim must identify whose projection it is. SCENARIO and SPECULATIVE passages must never acquire authoritative citations merely to make them look more certain than they are.

## Quotations and copyright

Quote only when the original wording materially matters. Verify quotations against the original or a reliable scholarly edition. Record exact page/section information whenever possible. Prefer paraphrase and synthesis for ordinary factual material. Never reproduce substantial copyrighted passages, tables, illustrations or proprietary charts without permission or a valid licensing basis.

## Claim ledger

Maintain a research ledger alongside the manuscript. Important claims should have a record containing:
- claim ID;
- chapter;
- exact or summarized claim;
- evidence classification;
- supporting source(s);
- contradictory/qualifying evidence where relevant;
- confidence;
- last verification date;
- whether the claim is time-sensitive;
- publication re-check required: yes/no.

## Publication audit

Before commercial publication:
1. Re-check every time-sensitive statistic and current policy claim.
2. Verify every quotation.
3. Resolve or flag unsupported claims.
4. Check citations against the actual source, not an AI summary of it.
5. Test URLs/DOIs and retain stable identifiers where possible.
6. Review controversial claims for credible counter-evidence.
7. Run copyright and originality review.
8. Produce a complete bibliography and chapter notes.

## Initial authoritative anchors

The research ledger should include, among many others:
- Library of Congress primary-source collections for industrialization and historical change.
- UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (adopted 23 Nov 2021) and UNESCO guidance on generative AI in education and research.
- OECD research on education, skills and AI.
- International Labour Organization research on generative AI and employment.
- World Bank research on digital development and AI foundations.
- International Energy Agency research on AI/data-centre energy demand.
- Stanford HAI AI Index for measured AI trends, with original benchmark sources used when a specific benchmark claim is consequential.
- International AI Safety Report for capability/risk synthesis, supplemented by original studies for important technical claims.

## Editorial principle

Sources are not decoration at the end of the book. They are part of the book's architecture. A reader should be able to distinguish what happened, what evidence suggests is happening, what an institution projects may happen, and what this book itself is asking humanity to consider.
